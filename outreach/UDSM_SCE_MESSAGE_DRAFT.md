## Email draft

**Subject:** Request to test an evidence-based decision method on a bridge maintenance case

Dear Head of Department,

My name is Brayan Lucas Mwangimba. I am a Tanzanian researcher developing an open project called Reality with AI collaborators. I am writing to ask whether someone in the Department of Structural and Construction Engineering would be willing to examine a small research method against a bridge inspection or maintenance decision.

The problem we are studying is simple to describe: a system can recommend an action before the evidence required for that action has actually been established, because missing confirmation, uncertainty, or shared dependencies between evidence sources can disappear as information is translated into a decision.

We have built a small prototype called a Claim–Evidence–Action Contract. It makes the claims required for a decision explicit, links them to evidence and known dependencies, preserves `UNKNOWN` when confirmation is insufficient, and then produces a disposition such as permit, revalidate, request human review, or refuse.

Our early tests found both useful behaviour and serious limitations. In one experiment using a public bridge-monitoring report, independent reviewers substantially agreed on the shared technical dependencies, but one reviewer identified a dependency in the analysis and then omitted it when converting the findings into decision rules. Our current prototype also depends on metadata whose completeness and authenticity are not yet solved. We therefore are not presenting this as a finished safety system or claiming that it is new.

We are looking for a technical partner who can help us test whether the method adds anything useful beyond established structural-engineering and assurance practice. A suitable first exercise could use a published, historical, simulated, or otherwise non-operational bridge case. It would not control a live system or replace an engineer's judgement.

Would a member of your department be open to a short introductory conversation? We would value help selecting one bounded decision, checking our assumptions, and identifying the fastest way to show that the method is useful, incomplete, or unnecessary.
