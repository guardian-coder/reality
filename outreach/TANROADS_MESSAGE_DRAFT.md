## Email draft

**Subject:** Request for a small, non-operational bridge decision-evidence pilot

Dear Director of Maintenance,

My name is Brayan Lucas Mwangimba. I am developing an open Tanzanian research project called Reality with AI collaborators. I am writing to ask whether TANROADS would consider an introductory discussion about testing a small decision-assurance method on one historical, published, or simulated bridge maintenance case.

The problem we are studying is that a system can recommend an action before the evidence required for that action has actually been established. Missing confirmation may be treated as confirmation, and several apparently independent sources may share the same power, calibration, communication, or administrative dependency.

Our current prototype is called a Claim–Evidence–Action Contract. For one specified decision, it records the real-world claims that must hold, the evidence used for each claim, the freshness and lineage of that evidence, and known shared dependencies. If the evidence is insufficient or contradictory, the method keeps that uncertainty visible rather than silently permitting the action.

This is not a request to connect software to a live TANROADS system, and the prototype would not replace the judgement or authority of TANROADS engineers. We are also not claiming that the method is already a validated product. The purpose of a pilot would be to compare it honestly with the existing process and determine whether it exposes a material evidence gap, adds unnecessary friction, or merely duplicates established practice.

A bounded exercise could proceed as follows:

1. TANROADS identifies one past, public, simulated, or non-sensitive bridge maintenance decision.
2. We map the claims, evidence, uncertainty, and shared dependencies involved.
3. TANROADS engineers review and correct the map.
4. We test false-permission and false-refusal cases against the existing process.
5. We return a short report stating what helped, what failed, and whether further work is justified.

Would the Director of Maintenance, or a member of the relevant bridge-management team, be available for a short introductory conversation? If this message has reached the wrong office, I would be grateful if you could direct me to the appropriate person.
