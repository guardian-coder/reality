# Vision

## Umbrella vision

Build infrastructure that lets intelligent systems interact with reality reliably.

## Long-range thesis

As intelligence becomes abundant and cheap, value may migrate toward inputs and capabilities intelligence cannot manufacture directly: trustworthy context, authority, institutional permission, physical capacity, verified outcomes, and accountability.

## The reliability problem

An intelligent system can propose a decision. A real-world outcome still depends on a chain of truth, authority, execution, evidence, and accountability. Failure anywhere in that chain can make the result unsafe, unverifiable, or economically useless.

## Current expression

Decision-Grade Truth means an evidence-backed claim about real-world state sufficiently reliable for a specified consequential decision. “Sufficient” depends on the decision, its error costs, and its evidence standard; it does not mean absolute or universal truth.

The first working domain is SME economic reality. This is a laboratory for testing the primitive, not the entire vision or a permanent market commitment.

The cross-domain hypothesis is that the same evidence-to-state problem appears in actionable intelligence, health, insurance, supply chains, and physical systems. Portability is not yet proven.

## Unresolved architecture

We do not yet know whether Decision-Grade Truth is the core primitive, whether Trusted Execution / Delegated Authority is deeper, or whether they are complementary layers in the same system.
